  READING.  One fixed intercept and one shared bandwidth reproduce an
  efficiency column that ranges over a factor of ten, to a few pp, with
  no per-family efficiency term anywhere in the model.  So the B.0.3 /
  audit efficiency column is very nearly a RESTATEMENT of bytes per
  dispatch.  It is not a kernel-quality ranking and the headroom it
  implies is not per-family recoverable slack.

3.7.1  FREE FIT: does the data choose rule 55's intercept on its own?
==============================================================================
  free two-parameter OLS of dispatch_us on unique bytes, n = 13:
    slope     = 3.841458e-06 us per byte = 3.8415 us per MB
                =>  implied bandwidth 260.3 GB/s
    intercept = 4.716 us/dispatch

  compare: campaign M4 ceiling 266.3 GB/s, my measured autotune peak
    262.96 GB/s, and rule 55's independently measured intercept 3.97 us.
  The free fit lands at 260.3 GB/s and 4.72 us WITHOUT being told
  either constant.  That is an independent recovery of both, from 13
  families whose only common input is a byte count and a census time.
    implied bandwidth is 97.8 pct of the campaign ceiling
    and 99.0 pct of my own measured autotune peak;
    the free intercept is 1.19x rule 55's 3.97 us.
  I keep the FIXED constants (266.3, 3.97) everywhere else in this
  report, because they are measured independently of this census; the
  free fit is only here to show the census does not need to be told.
    R^2 of the free fit on dispatch_us = 0.999528

==============================================================================
3.7.3  THE gate_sp COINCIDENCE -- an internal consistency check I did not plant
==============================================================================
  T2b  gate_sp h64 (30 dispatches, 262 KB each): slack 3.313 us/dispatch
  T2b' gate_sp h48 (10 dispatches, 197 KB each): slack 3.310 us/dispatch
  difference: 0.003 us

  These are the SAME kernel at two head counts, measured as two separate
  B.0.3 rows with different call counts and different byte totals, and
  the model leaves them with the same residual to three decimal places.
  A per-family efficiency story has no reason to do that.  A fixed
  per-dispatch latency pool does.  So the gate_sp family carries a real,
  geometry-independent ~3.3 us/dispatch latency pool ON TOP of the
  universal intercept -- which is exactly the one LATENCY verdict this
  census issued (section 3.3), now independently corroborated.

==============================================================================
3.7.4  PREDICTION: the model must FAIL on the attention families
==============================================================================
  Rule 100 (my #642) labels T3a/T3a' ISSUE-bound at 97.7 pct of peak
  instruction issue.  A bytes-plus-intercept model must therefore
  UNDER-predict their dispatch time badly.  If it fit them too, the
  model would be vacuous.

  The fair comparison group is the BYTE-MATCHED neighbours, not the whole
  census: T1a at 1.05 MB/dispatch and T2a at 1.11 MB/dispatch bracket
  attention from below on bytes, and attention has about TWICE their
  bytes per dispatch, so if anything it should sit CLOSER to the floor.

  family                       B/disp    disp_us  pred_us   meas/pred  verdict
  T3a  sliding fused attn       2.097M     21.20   11.845        1.79x  MODEL FAILS (as predicted)
  T3a' full fused attn          2.359M     22.97   12.828        1.79x  MODEL FAILS (as predicted)
  T1a residual/rms/router       1.049M      8.02    7.908        1.01x  byte-matched neighbour, model holds
  T2a shared gate+up            1.114M      7.36    8.154        0.90x  byte-matched neighbour, model holds

  The model under-predicts BOTH attention rows by 1.79x -- and it does so at
  two different head counts and two different byte totals, which is
  itself a second unplanted consistency check.  Meanwhile its two
  byte-matched neighbours land at 1.01x and 0.90x with twice as few
  bytes to hide behind.  That is the discrimination the whole-decode
  closure of section 3.6 could NOT provide, and it is a real prediction:
  a no-free-parameter bytes model separates the rule-100 ISSUE families
  from their own byte neighbours by nearly a factor of two.

  HONESTY, two ways.  (1) The gate_sp rows in the table above also miss,
  by about 1.7x.  I am not claiming the model fits everything except
  attention.  I am claiming its DOMAIN is bytes-dominated dispatches,
  and that BOTH misses are non-byte pools it correctly refuses to
  explain -- gate_sp latency (section 3.3) and attention issue (rule
  100).  The model is a bytes floor, and a floor is allowed to be
  missed from above.  What would falsify the census is a LARGE-byte
  family missing from above, and none does:
    over the 11 families at or above 1 MB/dispatch the largest
    meas/pred is 1.065x (T3c oproj h48), against attention's 1.79x.
    Every one of the 11 sits inside [0.903x, 1.065x].
  (2) T3a here uses B.0.3's published 636.0 M4 us/step.  Under the
  staleness correction I apply in section 3.6 (618.9) the dispatch is
  20.63 us and the ratio 1.74x -- the verdict does not move.

==============================================================================
3.7.5  WHAT THIS DOES TO THE CAMPAIGN'S HEADROOM RANKING
==============================================================================
  fern's audit ranks remaining headroom by (best_efficiency - own
  efficiency) x own_time.  Under 3.7 that quantity is, for the 13
  non-attention families, mostly a measure of SMALL BYTES PER DISPATCH.
  The implied levers are therefore not kernel-efficiency levers; they are
  dispatch-count levers, and rule 105.13 prices those at k_dispatch =
  1.89, not at alpha or beta.  Re-ranking the audit's top latency-looking
  rows by what is actually recoverable:

  family                  audit says (M5 us)   this census says
  T2b gate_sp h64                110.8         3.31 us/disp real pool
                                               + 30 dispatches to merge
  T1a residual/rms/router         87.6         at floor; only the 39
                                               dispatches are recoverable
  T2a shared gate+up              70.5         BELOW floor; nothing
  T3a sliding fused attn         212.2         ISSUE-bound (rule 100);
                                               no bytes lever, no k

  So of the audit's four biggest latency-looking pools, three are
  dispatch count and one is instruction issue.  NONE of them is kernel
  bandwidth efficiency.  That is the census's single most actionable
  structural finding.

  What this does and does not say about the arms on the board.  It does
  NOT say amortisation cannot work; it bounds what amortisation can win
  IF the byte traffic is unchanged.  alphonse #644 on A is bounded at
  0.45 bars, frieren #597 on B at zero or less, edward #629 on C at 0.03
  bars and on D at 0.72 bars (section 3.7.2, priced at beta).  Any of
  those arms that instead REMOVES BYTES, or removes DISPATCHES, is
  outside this bound and is priced at alpha or at k_dispatch = 1.89
  respectively.  The bound is on fixed-byte kernel rewriting only.

==============================================================================
3.7.6  PROVENANCE AND THREATS FOR SECTION 3.7
==============================================================================
  Every dispatch_us in 3.7 except family D is DERIVED, not measured by
  me: it is B.0.3's M4 census total for the family divided by the
  family's call count.  I never use B.0.3's M5 column, which is itself
  derived from the M4 column by a fixed ratio (the circularity trap in
  the assignment spec).  Family D is the one row I measured directly
  with my own dose ladder: 38.40 to 38.80 us/dispatch against B.0.3's
  1497.7/39 = 38.40, agreeing to +1.03 pct.  That single agreement is
  the ONLY licence I have for treating the other twelve derived rows as
  real per-dispatch times, and it is a one-family licence.  If B.0.3's
  M4 census is biased family-by-family, 3.7's residual column inherits
  that bias one-for-one, and the residuals are small numbers formed by
  differencing large ones: family C's 0.055 us slack is 0.12 pct of its
  44.67 us dispatch, so a 1 pct census error moves it by 8x its value.
  I therefore treat the SIGN and ORDER OF MAGNITUDE of the residual
  column as the finding, and I do NOT defend any individual residual
  below about 1 us/dispatch.  The two conclusions that survive that
  concession are the ones I actually report: no non-attention family
  clears one bar on fixed-byte slack, and the efficiency column is a
  restatement of bytes per dispatch.  Both are robust to a few pct of
  per-family census error because both are about the whole column.

  Second threat: PEAK.  I use 266.3 GB/s, the campaign M4 ceiling.  My
  own best measured achieved bandwidth is 262.96 GB/s (98.7 pct).  Using
  262.96 instead inflates every bus_us by 1.27 pct and so SHRINKS
  every positive slack -- for family D from 0.963 to 0.538 us/dispatch, and it
  flips 1 further family from positive slack to at/below floor.
  So the choice of 266.3 is the conservative one for a STOP verdict: it
  reports MORE recoverable slack than my own hardware measurement would.
  Every STOP in 3.7.2 is therefore a fortiori.
