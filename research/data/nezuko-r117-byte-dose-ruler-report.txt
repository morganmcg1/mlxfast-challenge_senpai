  If these rungs do not share one tau, then 'the byte class' is not a
  scalar: the marginal cost of a byte depends on WHICH kernel owns it
  and on how far that kernel already sits from the DRAM asymptote.
  Treat any ordering here as a hypothesis to be tested on new data.
  CONFOUND, found after launch and not pre-registered: no rung is a
  PURE byte dose. Every kill switch that resizes the plane also
  changes how the plane is addressed -- PAIRWISE=0 makes 32 lanes
  read 32 distinct scale bytes where the shipped kernel has lane 2j
  and 2j+1 share one byte, and NARROW=0 swaps a nibble walk for a
  strided byte read. That is why OP can exceed 1. It is also exactly
  the bundle an encoder change would buy, so the ruler stays
  ecologically valid for pricing an encoder -- it is just not a
  clean byte-count instrument, and must not be quoted as one.
