## 0. Verdict first

**P0 is confirmed, but not by the dichotomy the assignment proposed.** Three of
the eight published rates exceed their host's peak DRAM bandwidth under
independent byte accounting — exactly the bar the advisor registered. The fourth
(attention QKVO at 106.8 % of M5 peak) **dissolves on byte correction alone**.
No geometry conjecture, no cache conjecture, no instrument conjecture is needed
for it: it was priced with a stale layout epoch.

The surviving dichotomy is sharper and cheaper than the one assigned:

> Every rate derived by **duplicate injection** exceeds its host's measured peak.
> Every rate derived from a **GPU-timer census**, and every rate derived from a
> **receipt differential priced at the correct layout epoch**, does not.

Receipt differencing moves from the guilty column to the innocent column. That
matters, because receipt differencing is the only per-family instrument we have
on the official M5 and the assignment was about to condemn it.

**The fault taxonomy resolves to F1 + F3, and F2 was a red herring generated by
F1.** F1 (byte over-attribution, specifically layout-epoch drift) is present in
four of our published family byte counts. F3 (cache-served re-reads) is what
makes duplicate injection systematically over-report. There is no evidence for
F2 (the `nat` deflator under-attributing time): the `nat`-deflated variant is
strictly *worse* at reproducing M5 than the SPLIT=1 census, and the apparent F2
signature was the F1 error propagating through a ratio.

**Rule 70 ("the routed pool is DRAM-saturated and CLOSED") is FALSE as stated on
M4 and UNSUPPORTED on M5.** See §6. It should not be deleted — its *practical*
advice against another MoE-QMV codegen arm survives — but its stated physical
justification does not, and the 2.4 %-score "routed rate gap" that rule 76
derived from it is an artifact of comparing two families priced in two different
layout epochs. The gap that does survive is 7–10 pp, worth 48.7–69.7 µs/step on
M5 for T2c (0.74–1.06 % score) depending on which same-host reference rate it is
held to; §6 and §7 carry both.

**The one number the advisor should act on first is not in the assignment.**
The two independent validations of the M4→M5 map disagree, and the disagreement
is localised to exactly one family (§5.2). Resolving it is a single cheap probe
on the M5 and it moves every M5 headroom figure we publish by ~12 %.

## 2. P2 — the measured M4 Pro read ceiling

Three different M4 "peaks" were in live use in this repository. None of them was
measured on this host. Now one is.

| source | GB/s | % of measured sequential ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| theoretical (r94 census) | 273.0 | 103.8 |
| hardcoded `research/fern_r100_attn_probe.swift:71` | 266.3 | 101.3 |
| "roofline-measured" (`research/maple-fern-…ledger.md:940`) | 260.6 | 99.1 |
| tanjiro pool differential, published | **237.4** | **90.3** |

**Measured sequential DRAM read ceiling = 262.98 GB/s** (median of the 8 rungs
at ≥ 192 MiB unique footprint; range 260.93–263.26). That is 96.3 % of the
273 GB/s theoretical figure — a completely ordinary DRAM efficiency.

**Measured achievable ceiling with 64 KiB block shuffling = 266.80 GB/s**
(97.7 % of theoretical). Shuffled 64 KiB blocks are *not slower* than pure
sequential at DRAM scale — the `blk` arm runs at 100–102 % of `seq` on every
rung beyond the cache knee. **Gathered access at 64 KiB granularity costs
nothing.** I use 266.80 GB/s as the achievable ceiling everywhere below, because
the routed-expert banks are gathered, and this says gathering is not their
excuse.

Preregistered P2 interval [230, 273] — **hit**. Preregistered point estimate
250 ± 15 — **266.80 is above the band**. Reported as a miss, not rounded into
agreement.

### 2.1 The cache curve, and the death of "warm cache" as an explanation

| unique MiB | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 | 20 | 24 | ≥32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GB/s | 2583 | 1674 | 1511 | 1445 | 874 | 590 | 538 | 510 | 441 | 313 | 268 | 265 | ≈263 |

The effective last-level knee is at **16–20 MiB**, not the ~24 MiB / ~48 MiB
figures quoted in the archive. Above 20 MiB the curve is flat to 2 GiB.

This curve is the quantitative replacement for the phrase "warm cache". It gives
a **floor** on the DRAM-miss fraction of any dispatch whose unique footprint is
`F`: `E_floor(F) = 262.98 / B_eff(F)`, where `B_eff` is read off the curve. That
floor is used in §4.2 and it is the reason the F3 story is no longer a hand-wave.

### 2.2 The published 237.4 GB/s under-reads this host by 12.4 %

I replicated the exact tanjiro pool-differential instrument (256 MiB pool,
1-pass vs 7-pass, `Δbytes / Δtime`) on this host, with *my autotuned geometry*:
**259.52 GB/s = 98.7 % of the measured ceiling.**

So the *method* is essentially unbiased. The published **237.4 GB/s is 90.3 %**
of the measured ceiling, i.e. it under-reads by ×1.1238. The difference between
my 259.52 and the published 237.4 is not the differencing — it is the fixed,
non-autotuned dispatch geometry.

A delegated code audit confirms the geometry and confirms it was *shared*: the
published M5 (620.3 raw / 610.6 normalised) and M4 Pro (237.4) figures both come
from `laguna_inject_dram_sweep_u4_v2` (`LagunaRuntimeModel.swift:11804-11939`,
landed `a3c096e`, PR #27) with **256 threadgroups × 256 threads, 256 `uint4` per
thread**, a 256 MiB pool and a 1→7-pass differential. Same code, same geometry,
both hosts. Caveats recorded: the M5 number is *normalised* while the M4 number
is *raw* (raw/raw = 2.613×), the checked-in replica probe is not faithful to the
original, and "610" has two conflicting provenances in the archive.

**Consequence: our published M5 ceiling of 610.6 GB/s is very probably also an
under-read of the same kind.** Applying this host's measured correction factor
gives an estimated M5 ceiling of **686.2 GB/s**. That is a conjecture, not a
measurement — it is carried as an explicit sensitivity everywhere below and
never as the primary number.

(912 tensors, 21,561,408,512 B; BF16 3,825,950,720; F32 39,936; U32

Validation: parameterised to the **r94 census epoch** (group-16 attention

### 4.1 Why F2 is a red herring

The `nat`-deflated variant of the census (which was the F2 candidate — marginal
time under-attributing fixed per-call cost) makes the M4→M5 map **worse**, not
better: residual −12.77 % versus −6.63 % for SPLIT=1 (§5). If the census
over-attributed time, deflating it should have improved agreement. It does the
opposite. The F2 signature that motivated the hypothesis (marginal < census with
no cache story) was the F1 byte error propagating through the ratio: once bytes
are consistent, the marginal/census gap is fully explained by the measured cache
curve.

### 4.2 P0-4 / P0-5 — the E column, with a measured floor

`research/artifacts/fern-r101/p0-e-column.tsv`. `E` is the published
marginal-to-census efficiency ratio; `F` is the **per-call** unique footprint
(injection re-issues one *dispatch*, so per-call is the right denominator);
`E_floor(F) = 262.98 / B_eff(F)` comes from my own measured cache curve.

| family | per-call MB | E | E_floor | in band? | implied C = F(1−E) MB |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T0a | 0.0010 | −0.045 | 0.102 | no | 0.001 |
| T1a residual/rms/router | 1.0486 | 0.349 | 0.102 | yes | 0.683 |
| T2a shared gate+up | 1.1141 | 0.311 | 0.105 | yes | 0.768 |
| T2d down+residual | 5.0135 | 0.617 | 0.220 | yes | 1.920 |
| T2c routed gate+up | 8.9129 | 0.754 | 0.456 | yes | 2.193 |
| T0b QKV | 10.2825 | 0.741 | 0.485 | yes | 2.663 |
| T1c lmhead | 109.1830 | 1.111 | **1.000** | no | −12.119 |

**Spearman ρ = 0.9286 ≥ 0.80 — P0-4 CONFIRMED.** `E` is monotone increasing in
per-call unique footprint, as registered.

Two honest riders, both preregistered:

- The implied reusable capacity `C = F(1−E)` is **not constant** (0.68 → 2.66 MB
  over the physical rows). I said I would report that rather than invent a
  capacity, and I do. `C` is not a cache size; it is whatever fraction of the
  footprint survived the *rest of the step's* traffic between the two dispatches,
  which depends on where in the step the family sits.
- `T0a` (`E = −0.045`, 1 KB footprint) and `T1c` (`E = 1.111`) are outside
  `[E_floor, 1]` in opposite directions and for opposite reasons: T0a is pure
  dispatch overhead with essentially no bytes, and T1c's `E > 1` is measurement
  noise on a row whose floor is exactly 1.00. **Every family with a meaningful
  footprint lies inside its measured band.** That is the complete, quantitative
  replacement for "warm cache".

**P0-5 answered.** A Δtime is a byte rate only if the Δbytes are *compulsory*
misses and the Δtime is their *isolated* cost. Duplicate injection re-issues one
dispatch whose unique footprint is 0.001–10.3 MB — comfortably inside the
measured 16–20 MiB knee — so its misses are **not** compulsory and its Δtime
understates the DRAM cost of its Δbytes by a factor of `1/E`. A streaming sweep
differential moves 400 MB – 1.6 GB of footprint that cannot be resident anywhere,
so `E ≈ 1` **by construction**. `T1c` is the empirical proof inside our own data:
the one family whose per-call footprint cannot be resident is the one family
whose `E` reaches 1.

This is a **physical** rule, and it should replace the "DEFINITIONAL" framing at
`research/RESEARCH_STATE_ARCHIVE_through-round-21.md:1760`, which is circular
(it defines the marginal as correct and then uses it to price). The physical
statement at `:4578` is the one to keep.

## 5. P3 — the M4→M5 map, and the disagreement that matters

### 5.1 The map and its residual

Two parameters, both fixed before fitting, neither tuned afterwards:

- `α` = bandwidth scale = 266.80 / 610.6 = **0.4369**
- `β` = issue scale = 20 / 40 cores = **0.5**

Pools taken from the SPLIT=1 census with a 70 %-of-peak bytes-bound threshold:
**bytes-bound 6302.5 µs, latency-bound 1793.8 µs, unaudited tail 432.0 µs**
(M4 SPLIT=1 total 8528.3 µs).

Predicted M5 steady-state decode = **3866.8 µs/step** against the measured
**4141.5 µs/step** ⇒ **residual −6.63 %**.

Preregistered: "negative, in [−20 %, −5 %], point estimate −14.6 %". Sign
**confirmed**; magnitude inside the registered interval and inside my 15 %
"do not add a parameter" bar. **I did not add a third parameter, and I do not
propose one.**

Denominator provenance, since this has bitten us twice: 4893.7 µs is ranked wall
(`research/CURRENT_RESEARCH_STATE.md:946-962`); the amortised seed prefill is
752.2 µs/step = 15.37 % (rule 58, `:1787-1799`, `:1055-1082`); steady-state
decode is **4141.5 µs**. Price of a decode microsecond: **0.015228 % score per
µs/step**, i.e. **65.67 µs/step per 1 % score** (exact log-derivative 65.25; the
0.6 % difference is a rounding artifact in the published constant, not a
disagreement). Effective prefill weight 0.365.

Sensitivities, both worse, neither used:

| variant | α | predicted µs | residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| **primary** (α from measured M4 ceiling / published M5) | 0.4369 | 3866.8 | **−6.63 %** |
| geometry-corrected M5 ceiling 686.2 | 0.3888 | 3563.3 | −13.96 % |
| `nat`-deflated census | 0.4369 | 3612.7 | −12.77 % |

### 5.2 P3b — the two validations disagree, and the disagreement is one family

This is the finding I would put in front of the advisor before anything else.

The map was validated a second, independent way: against the two official M5
receipt differentials, family by family.

| family | M4 SPLIT=1 µs | predicted M5 µs | measured M5 µs | residual | M5 achieved | % of 610.6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| routed | 2261.2 | 988.0 | 1010.67 | **−2.24 %** | 515.9 GB/s | 84.5 % |
| qkvo | 3122.4 | 1364.3 | 1230.70 | **+10.86 %** | 597.9 GB/s | **97.9 %** |

Routed validates the map almost perfectly. QKVO misses by +10.9 % in the
*opposite* direction to the whole-step residual. The two validations cannot both
be right, and the discrepancy is **entirely localised to qkvo**.

There is exactly one hypothesis that explains both at once. On M4, qkvo achieves
88.3 % of the measured ceiling. On M5, the same family appears to achieve 97.9 %
— a family does not become 9.6 pp more efficient by changing host. **If the true
M5 ceiling is ≈686 GB/s** (the geometry-corrected estimate from §2.2), qkvo on
M5 achieves **87.1 %** — matching its M4 efficiency almost exactly.

So the qkvo residual is *independent* evidence for the fixed-geometry under-read
conjecture, arrived at from a completely different direction (family timing, not
probe replication).

**The α/β degeneracy this creates is the thing to resolve.** A single scalar
(the whole-step residual) cannot separate the two parameters. Both of these fit
the data we have:

- `α ≈ 0.389` (M5 ceiling 686) with an M5 efficiency term ≈ 0.86;
- `α ≈ 0.437` (M5 ceiling 610.6) with an M5 efficiency term ≈ 0.62.

They differ by ~12 % in every M5 headroom figure we publish, and they imply
opposite research programmes: the first says M5 is *less* saturated than we
think and per-family efficiency work pays; the second says M5 is near its
ceiling and only bytes pay.

**The one experiment that resolves it: run the autotuned streaming-sweep ceiling
probe on the official M5.** It is the same probe I ran here
(`research/fern_r101_bw_probe.swift`), it needs no submitted-surface change, it
takes ~7 seconds, and it replaces a conjectured M5 ceiling with a measured one.
I did not run it because I have no M5 and the arm authorised no receipt.

### 5.3 Why I trust the geometry conjecture more than I expected to

Because I can reproduce the *failure mode* on demand. §1's autotune shows a
sane-looking geometry (2 threadgroups/core × 128 threads) reading the same
hardware at 226.9 GB/s instead of 263.8 — a 14 % under-read from geometry alone,
on a probe that would otherwise look perfectly healthy. The published instrument
used a *fixed* 256×256 geometry chosen on one machine and applied to both. That
is precisely the shape of error that produces a 90.3 %-of-ceiling M4 number, and
there is no reason it would spare the M5.

## 6. P4 — the rule 70 verdict

**Rule 70 is FALSE as stated on M4, and UNSUPPORTED on M5.**

I preregistered "TRUE on M4, and the cited M5 support is invalid but
replaceable", and named in advance the two observations that would flip me. The
second one fired.

**On M4**, with consistent HEAD bytes and the measured ceiling:

| family | achieved / measured M4 ceiling |
|---|---|
| routed gate+up (T2c) | **87.0 %** |
| routed down+residual (T2d) | 85.3 % |
| dense gate_up (layer 0) | **93.4 %** |
| dense_down (layer 0) | **94.0 %** |
| lmhead (T1c) | **97.4 %** |

The routed pool is not at the machine's demonstrated limit. It is **10.4 pp**
below a rate demonstrated *on the same host, in the same step, by the same
runtime* — or **7.0 pp** below if you insist on `dense_down` as the fairer
same-access-pattern reference instead of lmhead. Either way, "saturated" is a
claim about a limit, and a family cannot be at the limit when another family on
the same silicon, in the same forward pass, beats it by seven to ten points.

**On M5**, rule 70's cited support was 546.2 GB/s "at ≈600 µs". The 546.2 was
computed with stale bytes; corrected it is **515.9 GB/s = 84.5 %** of the
published peak — about 13 pp below what the qkvo differential achieves on the
same machine. And the "≈600 µs pool" was never measured on M5: it was an M4
figure scaled by a wall ratio.

**The advisor's own counter-argument was epoch-mixed.** The claim that there is
"no meaningful gap (88.0 % vs 89.7 %)" compares a stale-epoch routed rate with a
HEAD-epoch attention rate. On his own denominators, with one consistent epoch,
it is **83.0 % vs 89.5 % = 6.5 pp**.

**What survives of rule 70.** Its practical instruction — do not assign another
MoE-QMV *codegen* arm — is still good advice, because five such arms have failed
and because a 10 pp efficiency gap is not automatically an addressable one. What
does not survive is the *reason*: "DRAM-saturated, therefore closed". The pool is
not saturated; it is 7–10 pp off a same-host demonstrated rate, worth **69.7 µs
on M5 = 1.06 % score for T2c alone** against the best same-host rate, or
**48.7 µs = 0.74 %** against the stricter same-access-pattern reference. Both are

## 9. Caveats, and what I did not do

1. **Host.** Everything measured here is M4 Pro, `applegpu_g16s`, generation 16,
   **pre-NAX**. Per `AGENTS.md`, an M4 Pro result is not evidence for an `_nax`
   change, and threadgroup geometry can change sign across core counts. The
   ceiling, the cache curve and the geometry-sensitivity finding are
   *host-general in kind* but *host-specific in value*.
2. **Rule 79.** The assignment cites a "new rule 79" from #555 (every per-kernel
   census contrast needs a same-session identical-code null). There is **no rule
   79** in `research/CURRENT_RESEARCH_STATE.md` at base `3567695b` — the highest
   is 78 at `:2051`. I report this as **documentation lag**, not a numbering
   gap: the content is real and I honour it. This arm runs **no per-kernel census
   contrast**, so 79 does not bind here. But the r94 census rows I consume
   **predate** it and carry no identical-code null. Every absolute µs in §7
   inherits that caveat; the *ratios* and *percentages of peak* are much more
   robust than the absolute microseconds.
3. **Escape rates.** I priced escaped attention rows at `e = 0` (no stock-plane
   re-read). Escaped rows do re-read the stock scale plane. Inverting the physics
   bound gives `e < 0.756` for qkv h64 before the row would become impossible, so
   even the worst case stays physical and no conclusion here flips. One decode
   step with `DARKBLOOM_ATTN_SCALE_NARROW_LOG=1` pins it exactly — a cheap
   follow-on.
4. **Dead code noted in passing.** The 3-plane `LagunaNarrowScaleBank` is dead at
   HEAD (`LagunaRuntimeModel.swift:5578-5583`, `:5496-5501`). Narrow-path
   selection is by array *shape*, so a failed certificate silently reverts a site
   to full width (`:8740-8760`, `:10566-10572`) with no trace. Not investigated
   further; flagged because it is a silent-fallback class of bug.
5. **M4→M5 ratios in circulation.** 0.456, 0.507 / 0.509 / 0.51, and 0.595 are
   all in live use in this repository and **none** comes from a paired
   measurement; `:1189-1190` already flags one as mixing conventions. My `α` is
   derived from ceilings, not from any of them.
6. **`research/pr80_receipt_analyze.py:35` hard-codes `M5_PEAK_BW = 651.8e9`.**
   That is the qkvo family rate, not a peak, and it is now known to be priced
   with stale bytes. Anything that script has produced should be re-derived.
   I did not change it (it is outside this arm's scope and touching it would
   invalidate prior analyses without re-running them).
